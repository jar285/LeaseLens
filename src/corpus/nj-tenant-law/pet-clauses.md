# Pet Clauses

## Lease controls; service animals are different

A NJ residential landlord may, as a matter of contract, prohibit pets, restrict pet types, or charge a pet fee. These restrictions are enforceable as ordinary lease terms.

**However**, federal and NJ fair-housing law require landlords to make **reasonable accommodations** for:

- **Service animals** (defined under the ADA: dogs trained to perform tasks for a person with a disability).
- **Emotional support animals** (defined under the federal Fair Housing Act, 42 USC §3604(f)(3)(B), and applicable NJ regulations).

These animals are **not pets** for fair-housing purposes. A landlord cannot apply a "no pets" policy, charge a pet fee, or impose breed/weight restrictions on a service animal or properly-documented emotional support animal.

## Pet fees vs. pet deposits

A **non-refundable** pet fee (e.g., $300 at move-in) is generally permitted for ordinary pets where the lease so provides.

A pet **deposit** (refundable) is treated as part of the security deposit and counts toward the 1.5-months cap (see security-deposit-cap). A landlord cannot avoid the cap by relabeling part of the security deposit as a "pet deposit."

## Monthly pet rent

A monthly "pet rent" of $25-50 is common and enforceable where the lease provides. Excessive pet rent (e.g., 20% of monthly rent) may be challenged as unreasonable.

## Common red flags

- A "no pets" clause applied to a service animal or documented ESA — fair-housing violation.
- A pet fee or pet deposit that, combined with the security deposit, exceeds 1.5 months' rent.
- A breed-specific ban applied to an ESA without an individualized assessment of the animal — likely a fair-housing violation.
- A clause that requires immediate eviction upon discovery of an unauthorized pet, with no cure opportunity — overstates the landlord's rights under the Anti-Eviction Act.
