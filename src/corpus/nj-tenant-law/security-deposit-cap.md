# Security Deposit Cap

## Statutory cap

Under New Jersey law, a residential landlord may not collect a security deposit greater than **one and one-half (1.5) times the monthly rent**.

The cap is set by **NJ Stat 46:8-21.2** ("Limitation on amount of security deposit").

A lease clause that purports to require more than 1.5 months' rent as a security deposit is unenforceable for the excess. The tenant may refuse to pay the excess, or recover it.

## Additional security after the first year

When the tenancy is renewed, the landlord may collect an **additional security deposit only up to 10 percent of the current security deposit per year**, and the total still cannot exceed 1.5 times the current rent.

## Pre-paid rent vs. security

A landlord may not relabel pre-paid rent as a "security deposit" or vice versa to avoid the cap. The substance of the payment controls, not the lease label.

## Common red flags

- A lease that requires "two months' rent as a security deposit" — over the cap.
- A lease that combines a "security deposit" with a "non-refundable damage deposit" or "cleaning fee" that pushes the effective tenant outlay over 1.5 months' rent.
- A "last month's rent" requirement on top of a 1.5-month security deposit — the substance is over-cap if the landlord treats the last-month payment as security.
