# Early Termination — Domestic Violence Victims

## NJ SAFE Act

The **NJ Safe Housing Act** (codified in **NJ Stat 46:8-9.6 et seq.**) gives a tenant who is a victim of domestic violence, sexual assault, or stalking the right to terminate a residential lease without penalty.

## Documentation that establishes the right

The tenant must provide the landlord with written notice plus one of:

- A certified copy of a temporary or final restraining order naming the tenant as a protected party.
- A police report documenting an incident of domestic violence, sexual assault, or stalking within the prior 60 days.
- Medical or counseling documentation from a qualified provider.
- Other evidence specified by the statute.

## Effect of termination

Upon proper notice, the lease terminates **30 days after** the next rental due date. The tenant is liable only for rent accrued through that termination date.

A landlord may not impose an early-termination fee, may not accelerate remaining rent, and may not require the tenant's continued payment after the termination date.

## Confidentiality

Documentation submitted under §46:8-9.6 is confidential. The landlord may not disclose it to anyone other than the landlord's attorney, employees with a need to know, and as otherwise required by law.

## Common red flags

- A lease clause that purports to waive a tenant's rights under the NJ Safe Housing Act — unenforceable.
- A clause that conditions termination on landlord "consent" — unenforceable; the right is statutory.
- A clause that imposes any monetary penalty for termination once proper documentation is submitted.
- A clause that requires a tenant to disclose the basis of the termination publicly (e.g., to a guarantor or co-signer).
