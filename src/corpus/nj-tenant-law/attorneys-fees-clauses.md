# Attorneys' Fees Clauses

## One-way vs. reciprocal

Many NJ residential leases include a clause requiring the **tenant** to pay the landlord's attorneys' fees in any action arising from the lease, with no corresponding obligation flowing the other direction. These one-way clauses are common but legally fragile.

A reciprocal clause — both parties liable for the prevailing party's reasonable fees — is the cleaner pattern.

## Statutory hooks

NJ does not have a blanket statute making one-way landlord-attorneys'-fees clauses unenforceable, but several overlays narrow them in practice:

- **NJ Stat 46:8-21.1** (security deposit return) entitles a tenant to recover **reasonable attorneys' fees** when the landlord wrongfully withholds the deposit. This is statutory and unwaivable.
- **NJ Stat 2A:42-10.10** (anti-retaliation) provides for fee-shifting in retaliation cases.
- The NJ Consumer Fraud Act (NJ Stat 56:8-1 et seq.) provides for treble damages and attorneys' fees in qualifying cases involving deceptive landlord practices.

## Reasonableness requirement

Even where a fee-shifting clause is enforced, the recoverable amount is limited to **reasonable** fees. NJ courts apply the lodestar method (reasonable hours × reasonable hourly rate) and may sharply reduce inflated bills.

## Common red flags

- A clause requiring the tenant to pay the landlord's fees in **any** dispute, regardless of who prevails.
- A clause that purports to disclaim the tenant's statutory right to fees under §46:8-21.1 (security deposit) — unenforceable.
- A clause that fixes attorneys' fees at a flat percentage of damages or rent owed — likely unenforceable as a penalty rather than an actual-cost recovery.
- A one-way clause combined with broad indemnification language (see indemnification-clauses) — stacks landlord-favoring fee recovery in two places.
