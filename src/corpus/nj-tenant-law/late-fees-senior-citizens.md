# Late Fees — Senior Citizens and Disabled Tenants

## 5-day grace period required

Under **NJ Stat 2A:42-6.1**, a residential landlord may not charge a late fee to a senior-citizen or disabled tenant whose rent is paid within **five business days** of the rent due date, regardless of what the lease says.

The protection applies to:

- Tenants 62 years of age or older.
- Tenants who are recipients of Social Security disability benefits, Supplemental Security Income, or comparable state-administered disability benefits.

## After the grace period

If rent remains unpaid after the five-business-day grace period, a late fee may be assessed if and only if the lease provides for one. The fee must still be reasonable.

## Pro-tenant interpretation

The 5-business-day grace period is a floor, not a ceiling. A lease that purports to shorten the grace period for protected tenants is unenforceable as to those tenants.

## Common red flags

- A blanket lease clause that imposes a late fee on the day after rent is due, with no carve-out for senior or disabled tenants.
- Leases that require senior tenants to "self-identify" or "register" for the grace period — the protection is statutory and does not require tenant action.
