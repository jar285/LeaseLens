# Landlord Entry — Notice Requirements; NJSA 2A:39-1 (Anti-Lockout Floor)

## Common-law reasonable-notice rule, backed by NJSA 2A:39-1

New Jersey does not have a single statute imposing a specific notice period
for landlord entry to a residential unit. Common law and the implied covenant
of quiet enjoyment require the landlord to provide **reasonable advance
notice** of any non-emergency entry. The statutory backstop is the NJ
Anti-Lockout statute, **NJSA 2A:39-1**, which prohibits self-help re-entry
and supplies the floor against which abusive entry clauses are evaluated.

The market norm in NJ is **24 to 48 hours' written notice** stating the date,
time window, and purpose of entry. A clause purporting to permit entry without
notice — particularly one that doubles as a self-help re-entry tool — runs
afoul of **NJSA 2A:39-1**.

## Acceptable purposes for entry

A landlord may enter the premises only for legitimate purposes:

- To make necessary repairs after notice and the tenant's reasonable opportunity to schedule.
- To inspect the premises (no more than once or twice annually for general condition).
- To show the unit to prospective tenants or buyers (typically only in the final 60–90 days of a tenancy).
- In emergencies (see entry-emergency).

## Quiet enjoyment overlay

Even with proper notice, repeated or harassing entries may breach the implied covenant of quiet enjoyment. The tenant has a remedy in damages and may, in extreme cases, treat the lease as constructively terminated.

## Common red flags

- A lease clause that gives the landlord the right to enter "at any reasonable time" with no notice requirement — likely unenforceable for non-emergency entries under the **NJSA 2A:39-1** floor.
- A clause that gives the landlord the right to enter for "any purpose the landlord deems necessary" — overbroad and inconsistent with the quiet-enjoyment limits of **NJSA 2A:39-1**.
- A clause that purports to allow entry with less than 24 hours' notice for non-emergency reasons.
- A clause that allows the landlord to retain a key for "convenient access" with no notice requirement — pairs with **NJSA 2A:39-1** concerns around constructive self-help re-entry.
