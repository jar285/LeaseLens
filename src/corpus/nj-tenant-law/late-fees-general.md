# Late Fees on Rent

## No statutory cap for general tenancies

New Jersey statute does not set a hard dollar cap or percentage cap on late fees for general (non-senior) residential tenancies. Late fees are governed by the lease itself, subject to common-law principles of reasonableness and the implied duty of good faith.

A clearly excessive late fee — for example, a flat $200 fee for a one-day-late $1,200 rent payment — may be challenged as an unenforceable penalty rather than a liquidated-damages provision.

## Lease must explicitly state the late fee

A landlord may not impose a late fee that is not explicitly authorized by the written lease. If the lease is silent on late fees, no late fee can be charged.

## Compounding late fees

Late-fee-on-late-fee structures (where unpaid fees themselves accrue further late fees) are generally unenforceable in NJ residential leases as compounding penalties.

## Reasonable per-day surcharges

A modest per-day surcharge (for example, $5 per day after a grace period, capped at a percentage of monthly rent) is more likely to be upheld than a flat large penalty.

## Common red flags

- Late fee that exceeds 5–10% of monthly rent for the first month.
- Compounding daily late fees with no maximum cap.
- A late fee on top of "interest at the legal rate" on overdue rent — likely double-recovery.
- A lease that imposes a late fee with no grace period at all (see late-fees-grace-period).
