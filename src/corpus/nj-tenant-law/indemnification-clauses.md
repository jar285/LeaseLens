# Indemnification and Hold-Harmless Clauses

## Common form

Many residential leases include a tenant-side indemnification clause: the tenant agrees to **indemnify, defend, and hold harmless** the landlord from any claim, liability, or damage arising out of the tenant's use of the premises.

These clauses are generally enforceable for ordinary tenant-caused damage — for example, the tenant's guest is injured by the tenant's loose rug.

## Limits on enforceability

NJ courts narrow indemnification clauses in two important ways:

1. **No indemnification for landlord's own negligence.** A clause that purports to require the tenant to indemnify the landlord for the **landlord's** negligent acts (failure to maintain common areas, failure to repair, etc.) is generally unenforceable as against public policy. The clause must be read narrowly to exclude landlord fault.

2. **No indemnification for breach of warranty of habitability.** A clause that pushes onto the tenant any liability the landlord has under the warranty of habitability is unenforceable.

## "Hold harmless" + insurance combinations

Some leases combine indemnification with a requirement that the tenant maintain renter's insurance naming the landlord as an additional insured. The insurance requirement is generally enforceable; the indemnification beyond actual tenant fault is not.

## Common red flags

- A clause requiring the tenant to indemnify the landlord for **all** claims arising on the premises, including from common-area defects or building-system failures.
- A clause that purports to make the tenant liable for landlord-caused injuries to third parties.
- A clause that combines a broad indemnification with an attorneys'-fees clause (see attorneys-fees-clauses) and a jury-waiver (see jury-trial-waivers) — a stack of landlord-favoring provisions that NJ courts may scrutinize collectively.
- A clause requiring the tenant to maintain insurance with coverage limits dramatically out of line with the rent (e.g., $1M coverage for a $1500/month studio).
