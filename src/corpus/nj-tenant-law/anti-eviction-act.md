# Anti-Eviction Act — Good Cause Eviction

## NJ Stat 2A:18-61.1

The **Anti-Eviction Act**, **NJ Stat 2A:18-61.1 et seq.**, is one of the strongest tenant-protection statutes in the United States. It limits the grounds on which a residential landlord may evict a tenant from a covered unit.

## Covered units

The Act covers most residential rental units in NJ, including:

- Apartments in buildings with two or more rental units.
- Single-family rentals other than the landlord's own former primary residence.
- Mobile home park spaces.

Owner-occupied two- or three-family homes where the owner lives in one unit are exempt from many of the Act's protections.

## Permitted grounds for eviction

A landlord may evict a covered tenant only for one of the statutorily enumerated grounds, which include:

- Non-payment of rent (after a three-day pay-or-quit notice).
- Substantial breach of the lease (after written notice and reasonable cure opportunity).
- Disorderly conduct, repeated breach of reasonable rules.
- Conviction of certain crimes.
- Owner's good-faith intent to occupy the unit personally.
- Substantial removal of the unit from the rental market.
- Sale of the building to a buyer who will occupy.

The Act explicitly does **not** allow eviction at the end of a fixed-term lease without a permitted statutory ground. The lease term ending is **not** itself good cause.

## "No-cause" eviction is unavailable

A common point of confusion: in many states, a landlord may decline to renew a fixed-term lease and have the tenant vacate at term end. **In NJ, that is not the rule for covered units.** The tenancy continues on a month-to-month basis after lease expiry, and the tenant cannot be evicted without one of the §2A:18-61.1 grounds.

## Common red flags

- A lease clause stating the tenancy "automatically ends" at the lease term and the tenant must vacate without cause — overstates the landlord's eviction rights for covered units.
- A "no-cause" termination provision purporting to allow eviction at lease end — unenforceable for covered units.
- A clause that disclaims the Anti-Eviction Act — flatly unenforceable.
