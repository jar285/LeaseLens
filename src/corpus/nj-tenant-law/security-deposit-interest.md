# Security Deposit Interest

## Interest is the tenant's money

Under **NJ Stat 46:8-19**, a security deposit on a residential rental in New Jersey must be held in a separate **interest-bearing account** at a federally insured bank or money-market fund. The interest accruing on the deposit belongs to the tenant.

Landlords with **fewer than ten rental units** must deposit security funds in a money-market or insured savings account paying at least the prevailing rate.

Larger landlords (ten or more units) must use an interest-bearing account meeting the same standard.

## Annual notice

Within 30 days of receiving the deposit, and at least once each year thereafter, the landlord must notify the tenant in writing of:

- The name and address of the bank holding the deposit.
- The type of account.
- The current rate of interest.
- The amount of deposit being held.

If the landlord moves the deposit to a new institution, that move triggers a fresh notice.

## Crediting interest

Each year, the landlord may either pay the accrued interest to the tenant in cash or credit it toward the next rent payment. Either way, it is the tenant's money — not the landlord's.

## Common red flags

- Lease language that purports to assign deposit interest to the landlord.
- Failure to provide the annual notice — the tenant may apply the entire deposit to next month's rent in lieu of suing.
- A landlord commingling the deposit with personal or operating funds (a separate violation under NJ Stat 46:8-19).
