# Month-to-Month Tenancy — Conversion and Termination

## Statutory framework

When a fixed-term residential lease expires and the tenant remains in possession with the landlord's acquiescence, NJ law converts the tenancy to month-to-month under the original terms. **NJ Stat 46:8-10** governs this conversion.

For covered units, the Anti-Eviction Act still applies — the landlord cannot terminate the month-to-month tenancy without one of the §2A:18-61.1 grounds (see anti-eviction-act).

## Notice to terminate (where permitted)

In the rare cases where termination is permitted (uncovered owner-occupied 2-3 family homes, certain seasonal rentals), the landlord must give **at least one full month's written notice** to terminate the month-to-month tenancy, ending on the last day of a rental period.

## Rent increases on month-to-month

A rent increase requires written notice equivalent to the rental period (one month) and must be a **reasonable** increase. Local rent control ordinances in cities like Newark, Jersey City, Hoboken, and Asbury Park may impose stricter caps.

A 30% rent increase, for example, may be challenged as constructively retaliatory or as a thinly disguised eviction effort.

## Common red flags

- A clause that purports to allow the landlord to terminate the month-to-month tenancy on **less than 30 days'** written notice.
- A clause allowing the landlord to raise rent at any time with less than one full rental period of notice.
- A clause that purports to allow rent increases "to market rate" with no cap, in a rent-controlled jurisdiction.
- A clause that converts month-to-month to a new fixed term automatically with no tenant option to remain month-to-month.
