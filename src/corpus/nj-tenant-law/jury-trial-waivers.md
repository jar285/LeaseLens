# Jury Trial Waiver Clauses

## Constitutional right at stake

The right to a trial by jury in civil cases is protected by both the U.S. Constitution (Seventh Amendment, in federal court) and Article I, paragraph 9 of the New Jersey Constitution (in state court).

A jury-waiver clause in a residential lease attempts to contract that right away in advance.

## Enforceability in NJ

NJ courts enforce jury-waiver clauses **only when the waiver is knowing, voluntary, and conspicuous**. The leading authority on this in landlord-tenant context applies a heightened scrutiny to waivers in adhesion contracts (which almost all residential leases are).

A waiver is more likely to be enforced when:

- It is set off in bold or all-caps in the lease, not buried in fine print.
- It is initialled separately by the tenant, not just covered by the global signature.
- The tenant had a meaningful opportunity to negotiate or reject the waiver — rare in adhesion contracts.

A waiver is more likely to be **un**enforced when:

- It is buried in a "miscellaneous" section without highlight.
- The lease is non-negotiable take-it-or-leave-it.
- The waiver applies broadly to "any action arising under this lease," sweeping in tort, statutory, and consumer-protection claims that have independent procedural rights.

## Eviction-specific exception

Landlord-tenant summary dispossess actions in NJ are generally bench trials by statute (no jury at first instance), so a waiver clause adds little for eviction proceedings. The clause matters more in damages actions, security-deposit suits, and habitability claims.

## Common red flags

- A jury-waiver clause buried in the boilerplate without separate signature line or conspicuous formatting.
- A waiver applying to "all claims arising in tort, contract, or under statute" — overbroad sweep.
- A waiver clause that explicitly waives consumer-fraud or anti-retaliation jury rights — likely against public policy.
