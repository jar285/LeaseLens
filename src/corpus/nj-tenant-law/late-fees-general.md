# Late Fees on Rent — Marini v. Ireland, 56 N.J. 130 (1970); NJSA 56:8-1 et seq.

The doctrinal foundation for evaluating late-fee clauses in NJ residential leases
is **Marini v. Ireland, 56 N.J. 130 (1970)** (implied warranty of habitability and
the duty of good faith), read together with the **NJ Consumer Fraud Act, NJSA
56:8-1 et seq.**, which treats unconscionable penalty clauses as unlawful practice.

## No statutory cap for general tenancies

New Jersey statute does not set a hard dollar cap or percentage cap on late fees
for general (non-senior) residential tenancies. Late fees are governed by the
lease itself, subject to the common-law principles of reasonableness articulated
in **Marini v. Ireland, 56 N.J. 130 (1970)** and the implied duty of good faith
recognized in NJ contract law.

A clearly excessive late fee — for example, a flat $200 fee for a one-day-late
$1,200 rent payment — may be challenged as an unenforceable penalty under
**NJSA 56:8-1 et seq.** (Consumer Fraud Act) rather than a liquidated-damages
provision.

## Lease must explicitly state the late fee

A landlord may not impose a late fee that is not explicitly authorized by the
written lease. If the lease is silent on late fees, no late fee can be charged
— a rule consistent with the contract-construction principles in **Marini v.
Ireland, 56 N.J. 130 (1970)**.

## Compounding late fees

Late-fee-on-late-fee structures (where unpaid fees themselves accrue further
late fees) are generally unenforceable in NJ residential leases as compounding
penalties subject to attack under **NJSA 56:8-1 et seq.** (Consumer Fraud Act).

## Reasonable per-day surcharges

A modest per-day surcharge (for example, $5 per day after a grace period,
capped at a percentage of monthly rent) is more likely to be upheld than a
flat large penalty. The reasonableness analysis traces back to **Marini v.
Ireland, 56 N.J. 130 (1970)**.

## Common red flags

- Late fee that exceeds 5–10% of monthly rent for the first month (likely an
  unconscionable penalty under **NJSA 56:8-1 et seq.**).
- Compounding daily late fees with no maximum cap.
- A late fee on top of "interest at the legal rate" on overdue rent — likely
  double-recovery.
- A lease that imposes a late fee with no grace period at all (see
  late-fees-grace-period).
