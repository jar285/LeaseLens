# Retaliatory Eviction — Tenant Protection

## NJ Stat 2A:42-10.10

Under **NJ Stat 2A:42-10.10**, a NJ landlord may not retaliate against a residential tenant for engaging in protected activity. Retaliatory eviction, retaliatory rent increases, and retaliatory reductions in services are all prohibited.

## Protected activities

The statute protects a tenant from retaliation for:

- Complaining in good faith to a governmental authority about a violation of housing, health, or building codes.
- Complaining to the landlord (or the landlord's agent) about an alleged code violation or breach of the warranty of habitability.
- Organizing or joining a tenants' organization.
- Exercising any other legal right, including pursuing the repair-and-deduct remedy or withholding rent for habitability defects.

## Presumption of retaliation

If the landlord serves an eviction notice or imposes a rent increase **within six months** after the tenant engages in protected activity, NJ courts apply a **rebuttable presumption** that the landlord's action is retaliatory. The burden shifts to the landlord to prove a legitimate, non-retaliatory reason.

## Tenant remedies

A tenant who proves retaliation may:

- Defeat the eviction action.
- Recover damages, including emotional distress and attorneys' fees.
- Obtain injunctive relief restoring services or rolling back the rent increase.

## Common red flags

- A lease clause that purports to require tenant complaints go through landlord-controlled "internal channels" only — likely unenforceable as discouraging protected activity.
- A clause that defines tenant participation in a tenants' organization as a lease violation — facially retaliatory.
- A clause that imposes higher rent or fees on tenants who file maintenance complaints.
