# Late Fees — Grace Period

## Common-law expectation

Although NJ does not impose a universal statutory grace period for general (non-senior) residential tenancies, a reasonable grace period of three to five days is the prevailing market norm and is the implicit expectation of most courts evaluating a late-fee clause.

## Federally subsidized housing

For tenancies subject to federal rent subsidies (Section 8, public housing), a five-day grace period is required by federal regulation, regardless of lease terms.

## What "grace period" means

A grace period is the number of days after the due date during which rent may be paid without incurring a late fee. It does not extend the due date itself — a payment on day five is still technically late, but the late fee does not attach.

## Interaction with three-day pay-or-quit notices

A lease grace period and a statutory three-day pay-or-quit notice (see retaliation-good-cause-eviction) are independent. The landlord cannot serve a pay-or-quit notice during the grace period.

## Common red flags

- A late-fee clause with no grace period for any tenant.
- A late-fee clause that imposes the fee on the same day rent is due ("late fee assessed immediately upon non-receipt").
- A grace period of less than five business days in a lease where the tenant qualifies as senior or disabled (see late-fees-senior-citizens — that grace period is statutory and unwaivable).
