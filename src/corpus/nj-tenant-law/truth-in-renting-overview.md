# Truth in Renting Act — Overview

## What it is

The **NJ Truth-in-Renting Act** (P.L. 1980, c. 233; codified at **NJ Stat 46:8-43 to 46:8-50**) requires every residential landlord to provide each tenant with a current statement of the legal rights and responsibilities of tenants and landlords under New Jersey law.

The Department of Community Affairs publishes the official "Truth in Renting" statement annually. A landlord must distribute it to every new tenant within 30 days of lease execution and post it conspicuously in any building of two or more rental units.

## Subject matter

The statement covers, at minimum:

- Security deposit rights.
- Habitability and repair obligations.
- Eviction grounds and process under the Anti-Eviction Act.
- Anti-discrimination protections.
- Retaliation protections.
- Privacy and entry rights.
- Lease provisions that are unenforceable in NJ regardless of lease language.

## Effect of non-distribution

A landlord's failure to distribute the Truth-in-Renting statement does not void the lease, but courts treat it as a serious procedural lapse and may consider it in disputes about lease enforceability. The DCA may also assess civil penalties for repeated violations.

## Common red flags

- A lease clause that requires the tenant to "acknowledge" they have read the Truth-in-Renting statement when no statement was actually provided.
- A clause that disclaims any landlord obligation under §46:8-43 et seq. — unenforceable.
- A clause that purports to override any rights summarized in the official Truth-in-Renting statement (security deposits, habitability, retaliation, eviction grounds).
