# Early Termination — Senior Citizens and Disabled Tenants

## Statutory right to terminate

Under **NJ Stat 46:8-9.1** ("Termination of lease by senior citizen or person with a disability"), a tenant may terminate a residential lease early without penalty when:

- The tenant or the tenant's spouse is **62 years of age or older** and is being admitted to a senior-citizen housing facility, a nursing home, an assisted-living residence, or a similar facility; OR
- The tenant has a **disability** and the disability requires the tenant to relocate to receive medical treatment or a more accessible residence; OR
- The tenant is a **victim of domestic violence** (see early-termination-domestic-violence for that companion statute).

## Notice requirement

The tenant must provide the landlord with written notice and supporting documentation. The lease typically terminates **40 days after** the next rental due date following the notice (timing varies; statute should be consulted for the controlling subsection).

## Liability after termination

Upon a valid termination under §46:8-9.1, the tenant's liability is limited to rent through the termination date. The landlord may not enforce a flat early-termination fee or accelerate the remaining rent.

## Common red flags

- A lease clause that imposes the standard early-termination fee on a senior tenant who has provided §46:8-9.1 notice.
- A clause that demands documentation beyond what the statute permits (such as a doctor's note for a relocation to a tenant's adult child's home that does not require medical care).
- A clause that purports to require landlord "consent" to the termination — the right is statutory, no consent is required.
