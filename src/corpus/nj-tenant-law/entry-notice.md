# Landlord Entry — Notice Requirements

## Common-law reasonable-notice rule

New Jersey does not have a statute imposing a specific notice period for landlord entry to a residential unit. Common law and the implied covenant of quiet enjoyment require the landlord to provide **reasonable advance notice** of any non-emergency entry.

The market norm in NJ is **24 to 48 hours' written notice** stating the date, time window, and purpose of entry.

## Acceptable purposes for entry

A landlord may enter the premises only for legitimate purposes:

- To make necessary repairs after notice and the tenant's reasonable opportunity to schedule.
- To inspect the premises (no more than once or twice annually for general condition).
- To show the unit to prospective tenants or buyers (typically only in the final 60–90 days of a tenancy).
- In emergencies (see entry-emergency).

## Quiet enjoyment overlay

Even with proper notice, repeated or harassing entries may breach the implied covenant of quiet enjoyment. The tenant has a remedy in damages and may, in extreme cases, treat the lease as constructively terminated.

## Common red flags

- A lease clause that gives the landlord the right to enter "at any reasonable time" with no notice requirement — likely unenforceable for non-emergency entries.
- A clause that gives the landlord the right to enter for "any purpose the landlord deems necessary" — overbroad.
- A clause that purports to allow entry with less than 24 hours' notice for non-emergency reasons.
- A clause that allows the landlord to retain a key for "convenient access" with no notice requirement.
