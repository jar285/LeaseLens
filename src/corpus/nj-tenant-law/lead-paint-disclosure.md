# Lead Paint Disclosure

## Federal disclosure (Title X)

For any residential rental built **before 1978**, federal law (Title X of the Housing and Community Development Act of 1992, 24 CFR Part 35) requires the landlord to:

- Disclose any known lead-based paint or lead-paint hazards in the unit.
- Provide the tenant with the EPA pamphlet "Protect Your Family from Lead in Your Home."
- Include a federally-prescribed lead warning statement signed by both parties as part of the lease.

The disclosure must be made before the tenant is obligated under the lease.

## NJ-specific overlay

NJ has additional lead-paint inspection and remediation obligations for landlords of single-family and multi-family rentals, particularly in older housing stock. Inspections are managed by local code-enforcement offices.

## Tenant remedies

Failure to disclose triggers federal penalties (up to $19,507 per violation as of 2025, adjusted annually) and gives the tenant a private right of action for actual damages, attorney's fees, and treble damages where willful violation is proved.

## Common red flags

- An NJ lease for a pre-1978 building with no lead disclosure and no signed warning statement attached.
- A lease that buries the lead disclosure in fine print or omits the EPA pamphlet acknowledgement.
- A clause that purports to disclaim lead-related landlord obligations — unenforceable under federal law.
